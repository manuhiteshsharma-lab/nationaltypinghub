Punjab & Haryana High Court Clerk Typing Test 2026: Complete Guide

If you have cleared the written examination for the Clerk post at the Punjab and Haryana High Court (PHHC), the next and most critical hurdle is the Computer Typing Test. Unlike many other state exams that require both Hindi and English typing, the PHHC Clerk typing test is historically evaluated strictly in English.

Many students fail this phase because they underestimate the pressure of the exam hall and the strict accuracy requirements. Below is a comprehensive breakdown of the official test pattern and how you can prepare to secure your selection.

What is the PHHC Typing Exam Pattern?

The PHHC Computer Typing Test is designed to test your raw execution speed and formatting accuracy on a computer keyboard. The exact parameters are as follows:

  • Language: English Only.
  • Duration: 10 Minutes.
  • Minimum Speed Required: 30 Words Per Minute (WPM).
  • Keyboard Layout: Standard English QWERTY keyboard.

💡 Crucial Rule: The required speed of 30 WPM is the net speed. This means any mistakes you make during the test will be penalized and subtracted from your final word count. You must aim for a gross speed of at least 35-38 WPM during practice to comfortably clear the net speed requirement.

How Are Mistakes Calculated?

Just like the SSC typing tests, the Punjab and Haryana High Court utilizes a specific formula to penalize errors. Accuracy is heavily prioritized.

  • Full Mistakes: Skipping a word, substituting a word with a different one, or typing an extra word that is not in the original passage.
  • Half Mistakes: Spelling errors (transposed letters), capitalization errors (using a capital letter instead of a small letter, or vice versa), and spacing errors.
  • Standard Word Definition: Generally, speed evaluation software categorizes 1 word as 5 keystrokes (depressions), including spaces.

Pro Tips to Clear the PHHC Typing Test

The secret to clearing the PHHC typing test is establishing strong muscle memory and keeping your error rate as close to zero as possible. Here is how you should practice:

  • Practice with Backspace Disabled: To prepare for the worst-case scenario, turn on "Strict Mode" in our simulator. If the real exam software restricts the backspace key, you will already be trained to type without relying on it.
  • Do Not Skip Lines: Missing an entire line of text while looking between the screen and a reference paper is the #1 reason students fail. If the test provides a physical paper, use a ruler or your finger to track the line you are typing.
  • Start with a 10-Minute Timer: Don't just practice 1-minute sprints. The real exam is an endurance test. Practice typing continuously for exactly 10 minutes every day to build stamina.
